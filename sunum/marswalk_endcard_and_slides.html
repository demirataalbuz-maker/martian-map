Slide 1 / 7 · Title & Core Shift

Marswalk Map

Target-first candidate landing site suggestion and obstacle-avoiding EVA route planner over a 20 m Mars terrain grid in the Jezero crater region.

1. Tap the Science Target

Instead of guessing an arbitrary start pin, planners select the geological stop they want to investigate.

2. Suggest Candidate Landing

Reverse Dijkstra suggests the closest suitable candidate landing site (L) plus separated backups (B1 / B2).

3. Plan & Verify the EVA

Plots least-time 8-connected A* routes, round-trip return legs, and SVG slope/elevation profiles.

Slide 2 / 7 · The Operational Challenge

Three Coupled Constraints Govern Every Mars Excursion

Lander Footprint Geometry

  • A lander requires an entire suitable circular footprint (guvenli_alan_capi_m) meeting slope and elevation limits, not just one flat pixel.
  • Primary and backup candidate landing sites must be spatially separated so one local scarp or hazard cannot invalidate all options.

Vehicle-Specific Mobility

  • Suited astronauts, crewed rovers, and robotic rovers obey distinct slope limits, obstacle clearances, and speed-vs-slope curves.
  • Steep scarps and mapped hazards require automatic corridor detours.

Life-Support & Walk-Back

  • Human EVAs have hard consumable caps (azami_sure_saat) including science work time at stops.
  • Crewed rovers are bounded by the walk-back survival rule if the vehicle fails at the farthest stop.

Slide 3 / 7 · Target-First Workflow & Three Traversers

One Mission, Three Traversers, Real Return-Leg Verification

Mission Capabilities

  • Candidate Landing + Backups: Prioritizes class-0 suitable candidates within range; falls back to class-1 marginal candidates only with an explicit warning.
  • True Round-Trip Check: Computes the actual A* return path from the last stop to landing/start and places a ! turn-around marker where budget runs out.
  • Resilient UX: Clicking an unreachable target preserves the active route and reports why even when the side panel is collapsed.

Side-by-Side Traverser Comparison

  • Walking Astronaut (suited): High maneuverability on moderate slopes, strict EVA life-support duration limit.
  • Crewed Rover: Fast flat-ground transit, tighter slope ceiling, range capped by walk-back safety.
  • Uncrewed Rover: Robotic exploration without human consumable time limits; click any row to switch instantly.

Slide 5 / 7 · Physical Honesty & Safety Architecture

True Mars Grid Math, Strict No-Data Safety & Zero Backend

Mars Grid Geometry

  • Zero Earth basemap tiles or Earth-radius formulas (basemap tiles up to z14).
  • All distances, times, warning spans, and the custom scale bar come from hucre_m (diagonal × √2).

"No Data" ≠ "Forbidden"

  • Unmapped cells (255) are hatched in legends, grey-shaded in profiles, and blocked by default so astronauts never enter unmapped ground unprompted.
  • Every parameter displays its engineering source & confidence tag (measured, scaled, ASSUMPTION).

23.1M-Cell Browser Worker

  • 256-entry speed LUTs + corridor-windowed A* (~11 B/cell, auto-expanding on border contact).
  • Streaming download progress; 46 MB Int16 elevation lazy-loads only on first route.

Slide 6 / 7 · Outputs & Reusability

Field-Ready Diagnostics & Drop-In Planetary Portability

Interactive Mission Diagnostics

  • Live 20 m Mars HUD: Instant cursor/tap readout of Mars coordinates, ceiling-quantized slope (with ≥ saturation flag), hazard class, landing class, and elevation.
  • Plain-SVG Profiles: Dependency-free Elevation and Slope charts marking stops, NoData gaps, slope limits, and max-time turn-around.
  • Mars GeoJSON Export: Downloads candidate landing sites, stops, leg metrics, and route line with explicit non-WGS84 Mars CRS metadata.

Zero-Code Adaptation to New Sites

  • 100% static single-page web app deployed via GitHub Pages—no server, no API keys, no login wall.
  • Switching to another Martian landing ellipse or a Lunar Artemis south-pole region requires replacing only the JSON metadata, Web Mercator tiles, and compact binary grids in public/veri.

Slide 7 / 7 · Limitations, Open Source & Transparency

Explicit Model Boundaries & Responsible Engineering

Documented Physical Limitations

  • Undirected Slope Speed: Speed depends on slope magnitude only; uphill and downhill are treated the same. Directional grade energetics and illumination/thermal shadow routing are next steps.
  • 20 m Grid & No Rock Layer: Slopes are ceiling-quantized over egim_taban_uzunlugu_m; meter-scale rock abundance is not modeled in the 20 m grid, so candidate landing sites require human verification on high-resolution imagery.
